Your SRA API gives firm data (who's regulated, firm type, offices), not rule changes. It isn't needed to track changes. Later, it can match each change to the firms it affects.

How it's gathered

Every morning, automatically. A person approves each change before anyone sees it.

Step 1

Check

A small script visits every source on the list.

Step 2

Compare

It keeps only what's new or different since yesterday.

Step 3

Summarise

AI reads the page and drafts the entry: what changed, who it affects, why it matters, key dates.

Step 4

Approve

Someone at Legl checks the draft against the source and publishes it.

Why the human step: reps will quote this to law firms about their own regulator. One wrong date costs more than the review takes.

How it's stored

Each change is one record in a simple database. Here is a real one.

One record per change

A consultation and the final rule it becomes are the same record, not two.

Updated, never overwritten

When the regulator moves it forward, the stage changes and the history keeps every step with its date.

Always dated

When the regulator published it, when it takes effect, and when we last checked the source.
